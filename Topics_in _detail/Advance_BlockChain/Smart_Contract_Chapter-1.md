---------------------------- Smart Contract -------------------------------

1. What is Smart Contract in BlockChain?
- A Smart Contract is a self-executing digital contract stored on a blockchain.
- It contains rules and conditions written in code, and once the predefined conditions are met, the contract executes automatically without intermediaries (like lawyers, banks, or brokers).
- They are immutable (cannot be altered once deployed).
- They are transparent (all participants can verify the rules).
- They are trustless (no need to trust a third party).


2. Inventor of Smart Contract
- The concept of Smart Contracts was first proposed by Nick Szabo in 1994 (a computer scientist, cryptographer, and legal scholar).
- He envisioned contracts as computer protocols that enforce the terms of an agreement automatically.
- Example he gave: A vending machine – you put money, select product → the machine automatically delivers, no third party needed.

3. History of Smart Contracts
- 1994 → Nick Szabo introduces the idea of "Smart Contracts".
- 2015 → Ethereum (by Vitalik Buterin) implemented the first practical smart contract platform with a Turing-complete programming language (Solidity).
- After Ethereum, many other platforms emerged:
1. Binance Smart Chain
2. Polkadot
3. Cardano
4. Hyperledger

4. How Smart Contracts Work
- Contract is coded (e.g., in Solidity for Ethereum).
- Deployed on blockchain (stored at a specific address).
- Triggered by events/transactions (e.g., "If Person A sends money → transfer asset to Person B").
- Automatic execution once conditions are met.

5. Example of a Smart Contract
- Case: Alice wants to rent Bob’s house using a blockchain smart contract.

1. Condition: If Alice pays 1 ETH by a certain date → the contract automatically transfers the digital key (token) to Alice.

2. If she doesn’t pay → contract cancels, no key is transferred.
3. No lawyer, no broker → trustless, automatic, transparent.

6. Key Features of Smart Contracts
- 1. Self-executing → Runs automatically.
- 2. Immutable → Once deployed, code can’t be changed.
- 3. Distributed → Stored across blockchain nodes.
- 4. Transparent → Anyone can verify.
- 5. Secure → Cryptography ensures tamper-proof execution.

7. Applications of Smart Contracts
- 1. Finance (DeFi) → Loans, insurance, decentralized exchanges.
- 2. Real Estate → Property transfer via blockchain.
- 3. Supply Chain → Track goods with smart contracts.
- 4. Voting Systems → Transparent elections.
- 5. Healthcare → Patient data sharing with consent.
- 6. NFTs & Gaming → Ownership transfer of digital assets.

8. Advantages Of Smart Contract 
- 1. Eliminates middlemen.
- 2. Reduces costs.
- 3. Faster transactions.
- 4. High transparency.
- 5. Reduced fraud.

9. Disadvantages of Smart Contract 
- 1. Code bugs = potential hacks (e.g., DAO Hack 2016).
- 2. Legal recognition still unclear in many countries.
- 3. Immutable nature means mistakes in code are permanent.
